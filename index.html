About

I'm an MS student in Civil Engineering at the University of Hawai’i at Mānoa, working at the intersection of transportation systems and cybersecurity risk. My thesis, “Understanding and Predicting Risks of Cyberthreats in Transportation Systems,” builds a structured, multi-modal dataset of transportation cyberthreats and pairs it with POLARIS-based dynamic traffic simulation to trace how a vulnerability in a single component — a signal controller, an EV charger, a roadside unit — becomes a network-scale delay.

Before Hawaiⅅi, I completed my Bachelor of Engineering in Civil Engineering at Tribhuvan University in Kathmandu, Nepal, and worked as a site engineer on a highway project before moving into research.

Outside the thesis, I'm interested in game theory, discrete choice modeling, and operations research — and how they can formalize risk and decision-making in infrastructure systems more broadly.

THESIS
2025–26
In progress

Understanding and Predicting Risks of Cyberthreats in Transportation Systems

A comprehensive dataset of cybersecurity threats spanning transportation modes and infrastructure assets — signal controllers, roadside units, EV charging equipment — sourced from vendor advisories, the NVD, CISA, and Exploit-DB. Component-level exploitation risk is estimated with a hazard-based risk model, then translated into network-scale outcomes (delay, disruption) using POLARIS traffic simulation on Oahu and Maui networks. Work has been presented at the Argonne POLARIS User Group Meeting and the UH Indo-Pacific Cybersecurity Innovation Forum.
